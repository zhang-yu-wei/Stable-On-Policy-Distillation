# Mixed-Policy Rollouts: A Survey in General Form

Survey note, 2026-08-03. Scope: methods in which the trajectories used for
training are **not** generated by a single policy. This covers interactive
imitation (DAgger and descendants), prefix/suffix splicing between an
expert and a learner, token-level interleaving in distillation,
hint-conditioned re-rollouts, off-policy traces inserted into RL groups,
and trained-emitter schemes such as Pedagogical RL. The aim is one
formalism that contains all of them, the axes along which they differ, what
is actually known about each axis, and (§5) the single objective under which
the emitter, the loss mask and the off-policy correction are chosen
together.

---

## 1. General form

Fix a prompt $x$ and an autoregressive process producing units
$a_1,\dots,a_T$ (a unit is a token, a span, or a whole agent turn; §3.1).
Let $s_t$ be the state (prompt plus everything emitted so far, plus any
environment observations).

A mixed-policy rollout is specified by three objects:

1. **An emitter set** $\Pi=\{\pi^{(1)},\dots,\pi^{(m)}\}$. Typical members:
   the student $\pi_S$; an external teacher $\pi_T$; a *privileged*
   self-teacher $\pi_S(\cdot\mid x,c)$ — the same weights with extra
   context $c$ (answer, hint, skill, hindsight summary); a stale student
   copy $\pi_{\text{old}}$ (replay buffer / asynchronous generation); a
   fixed dataset, i.e. a degenerate emitter that ignores $s_t$.
2. **A controller** $\kappa(u_t\mid h_t)$ choosing which emitter produces
   unit $t$, where $h_t$ is whatever the controller observes (step index,
   training iteration, measured student competence, teacher rank of the
   student's proposal, …). The realized behavior policy is
   $$\mu(a_t\mid s_t)=\sum_{i}\kappa\big(u_t=i\mid h_t\big)\,\pi^{(i)}(a_t\mid s_t).$$
3. **A persistence rule**: whether $u_t$ is redrawn every unit, held for a
   span, or drawn once per trajectory. Persistence is what separates
   "token-level interleaving" from "expert prefix, student suffix" from
   "one off-policy trajectory inside an on-policy group" — all three have
   the same $\mu$ marginal per step and very different trajectories.

Two consumption choices complete the design:

4. **The loss mask**: which units receive a gradient — all of them, only
   student-emitted units, only teacher-emitted units, or a learned/gated
   subset.
5. **The correction**: what, if anything, compensates for the fact that
   the data came from $\mu$ and not from $\pi_\theta$ — nothing,
   importance weights (with clipping), group/baseline construction, or
   per-token gates.

Equivalent framing used in the LLM-agent literature: a training method is
a triple (context distribution $p_s$, label distribution $p_a$, weighting
$w$); mixed-policy rollout is a choice of $p_s$, orthogonal to who
supplies labels (arXiv 2605.12913). The 2×2 "prefix source × KL
direction" table of Decoupling-KL (2605.16826) is the same statement for
distillation: SFT, DAgger, offline RL and OPD are its four cells.

### 1.1 Sample mixture vs distribution mixture

Switching emitters is not the same as averaging them. Three distinct
constructions get called "mixing":

| Construction | Definition | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Sample-level switch | draw $u_t$, then $a_t\sim\pi^{(u_t)}$ | $\bigcup_i\operatorname{supp}\pi^{(i)}$ |
| Arithmetic mixture | $a_t\sim\sum_i\lambda_i\pi^{(i)}$ | $\bigcup_i\operatorname{supp}\pi^{(i)}$ |
| Geometric mixture | $a_t\sim\prod_i(\pi^{(i)})^{\lambda_i}/Z$ | $\bigcap_i\operatorname{supp}\pi^{(i)}$ |

Sample-level and arithmetic mixtures agree in marginal distribution per
step but differ in trajectory statistics once persistence is longer than
one unit. The geometric mixture is qualitatively different: it can only
emit units that *every* component finds plausible, so it never produces a
token outside the student's support. That is why geometric blending
appears as a stabilizer when the teacher is far from the student, and why
arithmetic prior-blending fixes (e.g. AD-OPSD's
$\pi^*=U\pi_{\text{base}}+(1-U)\pi_T$) need an extra gate to get the same
protection.

### 1.2 What mixing does and does not change

Mixing changes the **state-visitation measure** $d_\mu$, not the per-state
learning target. Consequences:

- If the loss is a *per-state* imitation objective (cross-entropy or a KL
  to a target distribution evaluated at $s_t$), mixing is a reweighting of
  states. The minimizer at each visited state is unchanged; only coverage
  and the effective weighting of states change. No correction is needed
  for consistency — but the argmin over a restricted model class does
  depend on $d_\mu$, which is the entire point of DAgger.
- If the loss is a *policy gradient*, uncorrected mixing is biased: the
  score-function identity requires the actions to come from $\pi_\theta$.
  Corrections are per-token or per-turn importance ratios, and the bias
  they remove is bought with variance (§3.6, §6).
- Masking the loss to student-emitted units removes the *action-level*
  off-policyness exactly (conditional on $s_t$, those actions are on
  policy) but leaves the *state-level* mismatch untouched. This is the
  cheapest correction available and is what prefix-splicing methods use.

## 2. Why mix: the two endpoints fail differently

**Pure student rollouts.** State distribution matches deployment, and the
policy gradient is unbiased. Failure modes: (i) when every sample in a
group fails ($\hat p=0$) the group-relative advantage is identically zero
and RL receives no signal — the sole reason most hint/prefix methods exist
(2605.05112 makes the pass-rate the explicit control target); (ii) the
student cannot visit states it never generates, so behaviors outside its
support are unreachable by any amount of on-policy training; (iii) in
multi-turn settings compounding errors push the trajectory outside the
*teacher's* effective support, so even teacher-supplied supervision on
student states becomes unreliable — measured as trajectory-level KL
escalation coinciding with success-rate collapse (TCOD, 2604.24005).

**Pure teacher/expert rollouts.** Supervision is high quality but the
student is never trained on the states its own mistakes produce. Behavior
cloning error compounds as $O(\epsilon T^2)$ in horizon versus $O(\epsilon T)$
for interactive schemes that train on learner-induced states (Ross &
Bagnell 2011). Additional LLM-specific failures: teacher tokens outside
the student's support dominate the gradient without being learnable;
privileged-context artifacts (length, style, directness) transfer along
with the task content; diversity collapses.

Mixing is the interpolation between these, and every method is a claim
about *where* the interpolation should be state-dependent. The recurring
quantity is not correctness alone but correctness **and** plausibility
under the current student — stated explicitly as a product reward in
Pedagogical RL ($r=R\cdot G^{\theta_S}_{\text{spike}}$), as a KL-proximity
condition in the sparse-to-dense analysis (C2: the teacher must be near
the student in KL, 2605.12483), and as a top-$k$-overlap predictor of OPD
success in Rethinking-OPD (2604.13016).

## 3. The design axes

### 3.1 Granularity and persistence

| Unit | Where the switch can occur | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Token | any position | distillation-time interleaving (SKD), distribution blending (MiniLLM) |
| Span / segment | at chosen boundaries | targeted repair of a failing region |
| Turn | between agent turns | agent DAgger, teacher takeover, TCOD |
| Prefix/suffix (one switch) | one cut point per trajectory | Prefix-RFT, JSRL, reverse curriculum, AggreVaTe |
| Whole trajectory | per member of a rollout group | LUFFY-style off-policy traces in a GRPO group |

The binding constraint in agent settings is that a state only exists at
turn boundaries: you cannot fork or reset an environment mid-turn, so
control switches and credit live at turn resolution or coarser, even when
the *loss mask* is finer (see `teacher_intervention_blueprint.md` §2).

### 3.2 Switching rule

- **Fixed probability.** $\kappa=\beta$ constant. Rare in practice as a
  final design; used as an ablation endpoint.
- **Scheduled / annealed.** $\beta_i$ decays with training iteration
  (DAgger's $\beta_i=p^{i-1}$; the LLM-agent version initializes at $1.0$,
  decays $0.2$ per iteration, floors at $0.6$). Curricula over the *cut
  point* rather than a probability: Prefix-RFT decays the demonstration
  prefix length; TCOD-B2F starts with the teacher navigating to
  near-terminal states and extends the student's horizon backward;
  TCOD-F2B does the mirror image; JSRL shortens the guide-policy prefix;
  reverse-curriculum RL slides the RL start point backward through a
  demonstration.
- **State-triggered.** The switch is conditioned on a measurement of the
  current state rather than on the clock: student success-to-go estimated
  by forked probes, teacher perplexity of the prefix, entropy spikes,
  repeated actions. Handback uses the same measurement on the student.
  This is the only rule whose optimal setting is stationary across
  training steps (argument in `principled_selector.md` §4), which is why
  state-gated methods tend to beat schedules.
- **Acceptance test.** The student proposes, a test decides whether to
  keep it: SKD (2410.11325) keeps the student's token unless it is poorly
  ranked under the teacher, in which case the teacher resamples —
  speculative decoding repurposed as a data-collection rule. The mixture
  weight is thus implicitly a function of teacher-student agreement at
  each position, with no schedule at all.
- **Learned.** The controller (or the emitter itself) is trained. The
  pure form is Pedagogical RL: rather than switching between $\pi_S$ and a
  privileged teacher, it trains a single emitter by RL on a reward that
  multiplies task success by student-plausibility, then samples
  trajectories from it. This is *mixing by optimization* — the emitter is
  approximately the tilted object $\pi_T e^{r/\beta}/Z$ with a
  learnability term inside $r$, rather than a switch between two fixed
  policies. It is the only rule that can optimize the *content* of the
  off-policy portion instead of merely its placement. §5 gives the
  objective this emitter approximates.

### 3.3 Direction of the split

Two opposite arrangements are both called mixed rollouts and serve
different purposes:

- **Teacher first, student continues** (teacher prefix → student suffix):
  jump-start RL, reverse curriculum, TCOD-B2F, Prefix-RFT, hint-in-context
  variants. Purpose: move the student to a state from which its own
  success probability is non-zero, restoring RL signal. The student is
  still the emitter of everything that gets a policy gradient.
- **Student first, teacher continues** (student prefix → teacher suffix):
  AggreVaTe-style roll-in/roll-out, teacher takeover with handback, "a few
  teacher steps" augmentation (2607.04574). Purpose: obtain a
  high-quality label or continuation *at a state the student actually
  reaches*. The teacher's output is the supervision target.

The first fixes exploration; the second fixes covariate shift. A method
that needs both alternates them (agent DAgger draws per turn, so
trajectories contain both arrangements).

### 3.4 Emitter identity

The teacher need not be a different model. Conditioning-side mixing —
same weights, extra context $c$ — is the cheapest emitter and covers the
whole hint/hindsight/skill family: hint-GRPO-style partial solutions,
meta-hints that scaffold without leaking the answer (2510.09388),
language-adaptive hints (2605.22567), hindsight rewriting of failed
trajectories, and skill-conditioned self-teachers. Cost: the emitter's
distribution shift is *entirely* attributable to $c$, so privileged-context
artifacts (style, length, directness) are inherited unless subtracted —
the motivation for contrastive constructions that difference a
correct-hint teacher against a wrong-hint teacher.

Time is also an emitter axis: replay buffers and asynchronous generation
make $\mu$ a mixture of past students ($\pi_{\text{old}}$ at various
staleness). Formally identical, and the corrections are the same ones
(§3.6), which is why the async-RL and mixed-policy literatures keep
rediscovering each other's clipping schemes.

### 3.5 Loss mask

- **Teacher units only** — the interactive-imitation default: cross-entropy
  on teacher labels at all visited states, regardless of who executed the
  step (2605.12913 does exactly this).
- **Student units only** — teacher-emitted tokens are masked out. Removes
  action-level bias, keeps the state-distribution benefit. The prefix
  family's default.
- **All units, weighted** — requires per-token trust weights. Prefix-RFT
  applies an entropy-based mask on prefix tokens; Pedagogical RL's
  assimilation loss weights each teacher token by a sigmoid of the
  student's own log-probability, suppressing tokens the student finds
  implausible; evidence-mask methods keep only tokens the privileged
  context actually supports.

### 3.6 Correction

| Correction | Mechanism | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| None | rely on per-state target being mixing-invariant (§1.2) | valid for imitation losses; biased for PG |
| Token IS + clipping | $\rho_t=\pi_\theta/\mu$, clipped | clipping suppresses exactly the informative low-probability off-policy tokens; clip rate grows with position |
| Sequence / cumulative IS | product of ratios up to $t$ | exact trajectory correction, variance grows multiplicatively (2605.07331 argues the cumulative-token form is the principled middle) |
| Turn-level IS | ratio per turn, not per token | matches the granularity of agent decisions (2511.20718) |
| Group/baseline construction | insert the off-policy trajectory into the group so it shifts the *baseline*, not just the sample | LUFFY-style; no KL term needed |
| Gating | surprisal, entropy, or evidence gates on off-policy units | biased by construction; cheap and empirically the most used |

## 5. One objective behind the emitter, the mask, and the correction

The axes of §3 decide where trajectories come from. There is a single
objective under which those choices, the loss mask (§3.5) and the
correction (§3.6) are determined together, and under which SFT, OPD and RL
are three settings of the same three knobs. It also answers the practical
question raised by state-triggered switching: if the teacher takes over
because the student cannot make a decision, the teacher's span must be
supervised — masking it teaches only the continuation, never the decision —
but supervising it directly is a step of unbounded size.

### 5.1 The objective and its solution

Per state $s$, with anchor $\mu$, evidence $A$, and state distribution
$\rho$:

$$
J_\beta(\pi)=\mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\Big[\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)}\big[A(s,a)\big]
-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid s)\,\Vert\,\mu(\cdot\mid s)\big)\Big],
$$

$$
\pi^*(a\mid s)=\frac{\mu(a\mid s)\,e^{A(s,a)/\beta}}{Z(s)},\qquad
Z(s)=\mathbb E_{a\sim\mu}\big[e^{A/\beta}\big],\qquad
\beta\log Z(s)=V^*(s).
$$

Training is the forward projection
$\min_\theta\ \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\big[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^*\Vert\pi_\theta)\big]$.
The three knobs are the anchor $\mu$, the evidence $A$, and the states
$\rho$; a mixing rule is a choice of $\rho$, and the mask and correction
are whatever estimator of the projection the available data supports.

### 5.2 Evidence algebra

Evidence adds in the exponent, so targets multiply. Two classes behave
differently:

- **Ratio evidence** $A=\beta\log(\pi'/\mu)$ cancels the anchor:
  $\pi^*=\pi'$. It is unbounded wherever $\pi'>0$ and $\mu\approx 0$, so it
  carries no step-size guarantee. SFT and OPD are both of this type — the
  formal content of "imitation ignores the student".
- **Scalar evidence** $A=\hat A_R$ from a verifier leaves the anchor in
  place and bounds the step:
  $\lVert\log(\pi^*/\mu)\rVert_\infty\le 2\max|\hat A_R|/\beta$.

Combining them, $A=\beta\log(\pi_T/\mu)+\hat A_R$ gives
$\pi^*\propto\pi_T\,e^{\hat A_R/\beta}$ — the tilted-OPD target of
`advantage_tilted_opd.md`, read as teacher evidence plus verifier evidence.

### 5.3 Each situation as a setting of $(\mu, A, \rho)$

| Situation | anchor $\mu$ | evidence $A$ | states $\rho$ | target and estimator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SFT / off-policy demo | any (cancels) | $\beta\log(\pi_E/\mu)$ | $d_{\pi_E}$ | $\pi^*=\pi_E$; CE on expert tokens |
| OPD | $\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta]$ | $\beta\log(\pi_T/\pi_\theta)$ | $d_{\pi_\theta}$ | $\pi^*=\pi_T$; full-vocab forward KL |
| RLVR / GRPO | $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ | $\hat A_R$ (group-relative) | $d_{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}}$ | $\pi^*=\pi_{\mathrm{old}}e^{\hat A_R/\beta}/Z$; weight $e^{\hat A_R/\beta}$ |
| OPD + RL | $\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta]$ | $\beta\log(\pi_T/\pi_\theta)+\hat A_R$ | $d_{\pi_\theta}$ | $\pi^*\propto\pi_T e^{\hat A_R/\beta}$ |
| Teacher takeover span | $\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta]$ | $\beta\log(\pi_T/\pi_\theta)+\hat A_R$ | teacher-induced states | IS-weighted CE on teacher tokens (§5.4) |
| Hint-conditioned emitter | $\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta]$ | $\beta\log\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x,c)/\pi_\theta\big)+\hat A_R$ | hinted-rollout states | same, with the ratio small by construction |

The RL row is exact rather than an analogy. With a one-coordinate tilt (only
the sampled token carries evidence) the normalizer is closed form,
$Z=1+\mu(a)\big(e^{\hat A_R/\beta}-1\big)$, and the projection gradient is

$$
\nabla_\theta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi^*\Vert\pi_\theta)
=-\,\tfrac{1}{Z}\,\mu(a)\big(e^{\hat A_R/\beta}-1\big)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s),
$$

which is exponentially-weighted regression exactly, and the policy gradient
to first order in $1/\beta$.

### 5.4 The teacher-emitted span

At a teacher-emitted state the data are teacher *samples*, not the target.
Importance-weighting the projection from $\pi_T$ to $\pi^*$ gives the
per-token weight

$$
w_t=\frac{\pi^*(a_t\mid s_t)}{\pi_T(a_t\mid s_t)}
=\frac{\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)}{\pi_T(a_t\mid s_t)}\cdot\frac{e^{\hat A_R/\beta}}{Z(s_t)},
\qquad
\mathcal L=-\sum_t w_t\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t).
$$

Three consequences.

**The surprisal gate is derived, not heuristic.** The $\pi_\theta/\pi_T$
factor down-weights exactly the teacher tokens the student finds
implausible. Pedagogical RL's sigmoid gate and the entropy masks of the
prefix family are approximations of this ratio. Nothing is masked: every
teacher token keeps a nonzero weight, scaled by its distance from the
student and by how much the verifier says the action was worth.

**$\hat A_R$ at that state is the probe quantity already being measured.**
$\hat A_R(s,a_T)=\hat p_{\text{after}}-\hat p_{\text{student}}(s)$: the
success-to-go after the teacher's action minus the student's own
success-to-go at $s$ (`teacher_intervention_blueprint.md` §1).
Group-relative advantages computed from student rollouts are not valid
here, because the state was not student-induced.

**One step is bounded; convergence comes from repetition.** With
$\mu=\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta]$ the update is one step of soft policy
iteration, $\pi_{k+1}\propto\pi_k e^{A_k/\beta}$: $\beta$ is a step size,
the per-step KL is bounded by roughly $\max A/\beta$, and the fixed point is
still the teacher-informed optimum with no residual pull toward the current
student. This is how a student acquires a decision currently far outside
its support without any single large update — bounded steps, repeated, with
the anchor moving each time. The requirement is that the intervention state
be revisited across iterations rather than seen once, which is exactly what
DAgger's dataset aggregation provides.

### 5.5 What $\beta$ means depends on the anchor

- $\mu=\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta]$ (moving anchor): $\beta$ is a step size, the
  iteration is mirror descent / soft policy iteration, and the fixed point
  is the greedy optimum of the evidence. Use this when the student must end
  up making the decision itself.
- $\mu=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (frozen anchor): $\beta$ is a regularization
  strength and the fixed point is the tilted reference — deliberately not
  greedy. Use this when staying near a reference policy is itself a
  requirement.

## 6. What theory is available

- **Compounding error.** Training on a state distribution induced (even
  partly) by the learner is what converts $O(\epsilon T^2)$ into
  $O(\epsilon T)$; the DAgger argument is a no-regret reduction and needs
  the expert to be queryable at learner states, not the learner to be good.
- **Roll-in decides coverage, roll-out decides credit.** The LOLS
  quadrant is the sharpest statement of why the two arrangements of §3.3
  are not interchangeable: reference roll-in reintroduces the train/test
  mismatch; learned roll-out gives only local improvement; the useful
  combination is learner roll-in with a mixed roll-out, and it comes with
  a local-optimality guarantee rather than a global one.
- **Divergence direction interacts with the prefix source.** Forward CE on
  student-visited states admits monotonic-improvement and regret
  guarantees that reverse KL and JSD provably lack even against a strictly
  better expert (Distributional DAgger, 2606.05152); long-sequence
  distillation needs substantial forward-KL weight to avoid entropy
  collapse (2605.16826).
- **Signal existence in group RL.** With binary rewards the group
  advantage is non-degenerate only for $0<\hat p<1$, and its SNR scales
  with $\hat p(1-\hat p)$. This makes "restore signal" a *measurable*
  objective for the mixing rule: choose the emitter/cut point that moves
  $\hat p$ into the informative band (2605.05112).
- **Correction bias-variance.** Token-level ratios ignore the prefix-state
  mismatch (biased, low variance); full-sequence ratios are exact and
  high variance; clipping trades gradient signal for stability and does so
  unevenly across positions — late tokens are clipped far more often than
  early ones, so uncorrected clipping systematically silences the tail of
  mixed trajectories.
- **When the teacher is admissible at all.** Distillation-style mixing
  requires the teacher to be both reward-shaped and KL-near the student
  (conditions C1/C2 in 2605.12483); OPD success tracks thinking-pattern
  overlap rather than teacher benchmark strength (2604.13016). A mixing
  rule cannot repair an emitter that fails these.

## 7. Known failure modes of mixed rollouts

- **Support drift.** Student prefixes drift outside the teacher's
  effective support, so teacher labels on those states are unreliable
  precisely where they are most needed; observed as KL escalating with
  turn index in multi-turn OPD (2604.24005).
- **Unlearnable off-policy content.** Correct teacher tokens the student
  assigns near-zero probability dominate the gradient. Fixes are all
  gates: surprisal gating, entropy masks, top-$k$-overlap filters.
- **Diversity/entropy collapse.** Mixed-policy objectives converge onto
  the off-policy tokens that are *already* likely under the student and
  ignore the low-probability ones that carry the new capability; entropy
  falls faster than under on-policy RL, and pass@$k$ degrades even when
  pass@1 improves. Any evaluation of a mixing rule that reports only
  pass@1 is uninformative about this.
- **Hint shortcutting.** A trajectory conditioned on privileged context is
  not a solution to the original problem: the model can learn to continue
  from the hint without learning to produce the hinted state itself
  (2606.09883). Mitigations anneal the hint away (2604.07747) or require
  the unhinted rollout to be the one that is scored.
- **Artifact transfer.** Conditioning-side emitters inherit
  privileged-context style: shorter, more direct outputs, changed length
  distributions. Contrastive differencing (correct-hint minus wrong-hint
  teacher) is the standard subtraction.
- **Clock schedules go stale.** Any $\beta_i$ or prefix-length schedule
  indexed by training step is tuned to one student trajectory through
  training and is wrong for another; state-triggered rules condition on
  measured competence instead and are, to that extent, transferable.
- **Baseline hygiene.** A 2026 replication found that a family of reported
  mixed-policy gains over SFT-then-RL was attributable to bugs that
  suppressed the SFT baseline (an optimizer bug that dropped
  gradient-accumulation micro-batches, plus a loss-aggregation bug); with
  corrected baselines, plain SFT-then-RL beat the mixed-policy methods
  evaluated (2604.23747). Mixed-rollout claims should be checked against a
  correctly-tuned sequential pipeline, not only against pure on-policy RL.

## 8. Instantiation checklist

1. **Name the emitters.** Prefer conditioning-side (same weights, extra
   context) when it suffices — no second model, and the KL-proximity
   condition holds by construction.
2. **Pick the unit at the coarsest level whose value actually varies** —
   turns in agent settings, since that is the only place a state can be
   forked or reset.
3. **Pick the direction from the symptom.** $\hat p=0$ → teacher-first
   (restore signal). Student reaches bad states that supervision never
   covers → student-first (label learner-induced states).
4. **Prefer a state trigger to a schedule** when any competence
   measurement is available (probe success-to-go, teacher perplexity of
   the prefix, group pass rate); fall back to a schedule only when the
   environment cannot be forked.
5. **Decide the mask before the correction.** Masking to student-emitted
   units is the cheapest correct option, but it teaches only the
   continuation, never the decision — so it is wrong whenever the student
   has to acquire the intervened-on step itself. In that case supervise the
   off-policy units with the derived weight of §5.4 rather than a hand-set
   gate; the remaining free parameter is then $\beta$, which has units and
   a step-size interpretation.
6. **Report pass@$k$ and an entropy statistic**, not only pass@1 — the
   dominant failure of mixing is invisible in pass@1.
